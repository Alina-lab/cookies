Supplementary Table S1 Descriptive vocabulary and definitions used by trained panelists to evaluate biscuits
Attribute |  Definition / Reference | Anchors
Appearance | 
Surface colour | Degree of surface colour darkness (top surface) | Light to Dark
Base colour | Degree of base colour darkness (bottom surface) | Light to Dark
Smoothness of surface | The appearance of the top surface from rough to smooth | Not to Very
Evenness of Colour | Whether the overall colour of the biscuit was uneven or even (uniform) | Not to Very
Smoothness of sides | The appearance of the sides of the biscuit from rough to smooth | Not to Very
Brown spots in crumb | The extent to which the centre (crumb) of the biscuit has patches (unevenness) of brown colour | None to Lots
Aroma  | 
Sweet | Aroma of demerara sugar  | Not to Very
Burnt | Aroma of burnt / charred food | Not to Very
Buttery | Aroma of Melted butter | Not to Very
Floury | Aroma of Flour / Uncooked Dough | Not to Very
Cabbage | Aroma of Cooked Cabbage (sulfury) | Not to Very
Baked | Aroma of ice cream waffle cones | Not to Very
Mouthfeel on First Bite |  | 
Firmness of Bite | The force perceived when first biting into the biscuit  | Soft to Hard
Mouthfeel on Second Bite |  | 
Density of Mouthfeel | The perception of the density of the biscuit in the mouth | Not (light) to Very (dense)
Taste and Flavour (after third bite) |  | 
Sweet | Fundamental taste sensation typical of sucrose  | Not to Very
Salty | Fundamental taste sensation typical of sodium chloride | Not to Very
Bitter | Fundamental taste sensation typical of quinine | Not to Very
Burnt | The same as aroma but perceived orally | Not to Very
Buttery | The same as aroma but perceived orally | Not to Very
Floury | The same as aroma but perceived orally | Not to Very
Baked | The same as aroma but perceived orally | Not to Very
Metallic | Fundamental flavour sensation typical of iron sulfate | Not to Very
Caramel  | The flavour of caramel syrup | Not to Very
Mouthfeel on Fourth Bite | 
Crumbly | The extent to which the biscuit disintegrates into small pieces (crumbs) on chewing | Not to Very
Crunchy
 | The product is hard to break down (high level of force) and divides when chewed with the back molars, while produces a loud (characteristic) noise/sound, ranging from not to very.  | Not to Very

Pasting | The extent to which the biscuit forms a cohesive paste on chewing | Not to Very
Floury | The perception of a fine powdery flour in the mouth | Not to Very
Granular | The perception of a large granular particles in the mouth | Not to Very
Rate of Clearance | The rate to which the sample clear from the mouth on swallowing | Slow to Fast
Aftereffects  |  | 
Sweet | As for flavour but after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Salty | As for flavour but after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Bitter | As for flavour but after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Burnt | As for flavour but after swallowing the biscuit | Low to Strong
Sour | Fundamental taste sensation typical of citric acid after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Liquorice | Flavour of liquorice after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Salivating | The amount of saliva production after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Drying | The feeling of dryness in the mouth after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Numbing | A feeling of loss of sensation on the tongue after swallowing the biscuit | Not to Very
Tooth packing | Degree to which the biscuit remains stuck to the surface of teeth after swallowing | Not to Very
